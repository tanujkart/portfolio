## Problem

The projects page is the highest-leverage page on the site and does the least work of any page. Thirteen projects render as equal-weight text cards: no images, no hierarchy, scrambled ordering, three cards with no verifiable link at all, and a five-color category system with no legend and internally inconsistent assignments. A visitor gets under 30 seconds and cannot tell which project matters.

Target outcome: a visitor (recruiter, admissions, VC, prospective collaborator) lands and within 30 seconds knows (1) this person ships real things, (2) which specific one is the impressive one, (3) how to verify it.

## Landscape (three-layer synthesis)

- **Layer 1 (tried and true):** grid earns the click, detail page does the persuading.
- **Layer 2 (search):** portfolios with project context and process documentation convert ~40% better than image-only galleries; 78% of decision-makers trust case studies carrying specific metrics over general claims; recruiters give a first visit under 30 seconds; 60%+ of first visits are mobile.
- **Layer 3 (first principles):** conventional advice says write a case study per project. That is why detail pages never ship. The binding constraint here is not code — `/projects/[slug]` is 20 minutes of work — it is the human writing of 13 case studies. Build the infrastructure for all 13, populate it for the 4 that earn it, let the rest degrade to today's behavior. A plan whose ambitious version depends on 13 units of human writing stalls at 4 and rots.

## Vision

### 10x Check

The page stops describing evidence and becomes evidence. Nobody remembers reading "interactive eutrophication model that drives a virtual lake toward a toxic bloom." They remember dragging a fertilizer slider on your website and watching dissolved oxygen crash and fish die. Two of these projects are already interactive programs; one of them can run inline.

### Resolved conflict

E4 (links inside cards) collided with Approach C (whole card clickable). Nesting links inside a link is invalid HTML and ambiguous to use.

**Resolution: stretched-link pattern (option A).** The card's detail-page link uses an `::after` overlay covering the card; the receipts row sits on a higher `z-index` and stays independently clickable. Valid HTML, one card link plus N receipt links announced to screen readers, full-card touch target on mobile. Accepted cost: text selection inside the card is awkward.

**Resolution (stated by default, not objected to): when a filter is active, the featured/compact tiering collapses into a single flat list.** Otherwise filtering to `hardware` could leave one lonely card in a four-wide featured grid.

Deliberate non-decisions, with rationale:
- **One `ProjectCard` with a `variant` prop**, not `ProjectCard` + `ProjectRow`. Two components sharing 80% of their markup is a DRY violation waiting to happen.
- **Filtering via `searchParams`**, not `useState`. Filtered views become shareable URLs, the back button works, and the page needs zero client JavaScript. A `useState` filter would force `"use client"` on the whole page and break both.
